# Pages read straight from the stores, every scan:   URL | price | label     (price and label are optional)
#
# PRODUCT PAGE   The price is your TARGET: you are alerted when the product reaches it. It also alerts
#                when the product falls 50% or more below the price this monitor has been seeing for it.
#
# CATEGORY or SEARCH PAGE   The price is a FLOOR: "nothing on this page should cost this little".
#                Use a category sorted cheapest-first. A Rs 60,000 laptop mispriced at Rs 600 becomes the
#                cheapest laptop on the store, so it shows up at the top of that one page. This is how a
#                price error is caught at the store itself, with no deal channel involved.
#                  - The first read of a page only records what is normal on it. After that, anything NEW
#                    at or under the floor alerts, and so does anything that FALLS to it.
#                  - A NEW item under the floor counts only if its list price (MRP) is at least 3x the
#                    floor (FLOOR_MRP_RATIO): toy laptops and phone cases stay quiet.
#                  - Accessories, spares and refurbished units are skipped.
#                  - Every product on the page is also checked against traps.txt and its own price history:
#                    a fall of 50% or more from the price it was seen at alerts quietly, loudly from 65%,
#                    at max priority from 80% (WATCH_DROP_PCT, URGENT_DROP_PCT).
#                  - A product the monitor has never seen, NEW on the page above the floor, alerts when its
#                    own card shows 85% or more off a list price of at least 3x the floor
#                    (NEW_LISTING_DISCOUNT): high priority, max priority from 95%. This is what catches a
#                    dropped digit, a Rs 74,990 laptop at Rs 7,499. Ordinary no-name listings on these pages
#                    show 50-70% off, and whatever was on the page at the first read never alerts this way.
#                Pick the floor well below the cheapest real product of that category. --doctor shows the
#                cheapest item on each page and warns if ordinary products already sit under your floor.
#
# Pace: one store is asked for a page only every 6-10 seconds, so about 6 pages per store per minute.
# A longer list still works; each page just comes round less often.
#
# To be confirmed by  python -m lootwatch --doctor  on the machine that will run this:
#   - Amazon and Flipkart often refuse cloud-server addresses (CAPTCHA / HTTP 403). When they do, the
#     monitor backs off and reports the page as failing instead of pretending it is fine.
#   - Blinkit, Zepto and Swiggy Instamart build their pages in the app for your delivery location, so a
#     plain page fetch usually finds no prices.
